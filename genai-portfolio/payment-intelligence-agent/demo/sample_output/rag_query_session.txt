  Q1: What are the requirements for storing cardholder data?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Answer:
    PCI DSS v4.0 mandates that storage of cardholder data be minimized and
    that all stored Primary Account Numbers (PAN) be rendered unreadable.
    Acceptable approaches include strong one-way hashing with per-record
    salts, truncation (maximum first-six and last-four digits visible),
    index tokens with secure pads, and strong cryptography with associated
    key management. Sensitive Authentication Data (full track data, CVV2,
    PIN/PIN block) MUST NOT be stored after authorization, even if encrypted.
    Documented retention and disposal policies are required, with quarterly
    purges of data exceeding the defined retention period.

  Relevant Requirements: Req 3.2, Req 3.3, Req 3.5

  Q2: How should encryption keys be managed?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Answer:
    Cryptographic keys used to protect stored account data require strong
    lifecycle controls under Requirement 3.7. Specifically: keys must be
    generated using strong cryptography, stored in the fewest possible
    locations, distributed only to custodians with documented split
    knowledge, rotated at the end of the defined cryptoperiod (typically
    annually for symmetric DEKs), and retired or replaced when integrity
    is suspected. Key custodians must formally acknowledge their
    responsibilities. Hardware Security Modules (HSMs) are recommended
    for key storage in production environments.

  Relevant Requirements: Req 3.6, Req 3.7

  Q3: What does PCI DSS say about access control and authentication?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Answer:
    Requirements 7 and 8 establish least-privilege access and strong
    authentication. Access to system components and cardholder data must
    be restricted by business need-to-know, with default-deny access
    control systems. All users must have a unique ID. Multi-factor
    authentication (MFA) is required for all non-console administrative
    access AND all remote access into the cardholder data environment
    (CDE), including from internal networks. Passwords must be at least
    12 characters, changed on first use, and locked after 10 failed
    attempts. Service account credentials must be rotated annually and
    used only by the service.

  Relevant Requirements: Req 7.1, Req 7.2, Req 8.2, Req 8.3, Req 8.4

  Q4: What are the requirements for penetration testing?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Answer:
    Requirement 11.4 mandates internal and external penetration testing
    at least annually and after any significant infrastructure or
    application change. Tests must cover the entire CDE perimeter and
    critical systems, must follow an industry-accepted methodology
    (e.g., NIST SP 800-115, OWASP), and must include both network-layer
    and application-layer testing. Exploitable vulnerabilities and
    security weaknesses found must be corrected and re-tested. Segmentation
    controls used to reduce CDE scope must additionally be validated by
    pen-testing at least every 12 months.

  Relevant Requirements: Req 11.4, Req 11.4.1, Req 11.4.5

  Sources:

  Q5: How should audit logs be managed and monitored?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Answer:
    Requirement 10 covers audit logging end-to-end. Logs must capture user
    access to system components and CHD, all actions by privileged users,
    invalid access attempts, identification/authentication mechanisms,
    initialization of audit logs, and creation/deletion of system-level
    objects. Log records must include user ID, event type, timestamp,
    success/failure indicator, origin, and affected resource. Logs must
    be reviewed at least daily (automated tools acceptable), retained for
    at least one year with the most recent three months immediately
    available, and protected from unauthorized modification using
    file-integrity monitoring or write-once media.

  Relevant Requirements: Req 10.2, Req 10.3, Req 10.5, Req 10.7

  Q6: What is the customized approach in PCI DSS v4.0?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Answer:
    PCI DSS v4.0 introduces a Customized Approach as an alternative to the
    Defined Approach. Under the Customized Approach, an entity defines
    and implements its own controls to meet a Customized Approach
    Objective stated for each requirement, then provides evidence those
    controls operate effectively. A Targeted Risk Analysis is mandatory
    per requirement, and assessor validation by a Qualified Security
    Assessor (QSA) is required at the entity's annual assessment. The
    Customized Approach is intended for mature organizations that wish
    to apply innovative controls equivalent in effectiveness to the
    Defined Approach.

  Relevant Requirements: Req 12.3.2 (Customized Approach), Appendix E
